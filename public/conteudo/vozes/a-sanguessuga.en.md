The speaker is Agur, son of Jakeh, and his words are addressed to Ithiel and Ucal ([[Pv 30.1|Proverbs 30:1]]). Agur carries a message of a prophetic character: some versions translate the original word as “oracle.”

He does not begin by talking about his qualities, but about his limits: he confesses that he is brutish and without a man’s understanding ([[Pv 30.2|Proverbs 30:2]]). Before God he sees himself as unable: he has not learned wisdom, nor does he know the Holy One ([[Pv 30.3|Proverbs 30:3]]). And he asks: “Who has ascended up into heaven, and descended?” ([[Pv 30.4|Proverbs 30:4]]).

But Agur’s limits do not keep him from being an instrument of God. Among the many subjects he took up, he spoke about the leech.

:::versiculo Pv 30.15 | Proverbs 30:15

## What is a leech?

The leech is an animal that usually lives in water, very much like a worm, and belongs to the group of the annelids. It is known for sticking to the skin of other animals and sucking. Its mouth is made to pierce and cut the skin and, depending on the species, it produces hirudin, a substance that makes the blood harder to clot.

:::figura sanguessuga
The leech: a soft body in rings, with a sucker at each end. Illustration created with artificial intelligence for the app.
:::

In plain words: it lives on the blood of others. It needs a host, and I believe it will not be you.

The leech wants to settle into a person’s body to unbalance it and take away its control. In the end, the body falls to the ground lifeless.

That is why it stands for a group of parasite people, who produce nothing but like to suck from others. The verse just before speaks of such people: teeth like swords, to devour the poor ([[Pv 30.14|Proverbs 30:14]]).

:::nota Facts about the leech
The leech belongs to the subclass of the hirudineans, within the group of the annelids: worms whose bodies are made of rings. Not every species has jaws.
The saliva of the medicinal leech contains hirudin, which keeps blood from clotting. The bite usually does not hurt: the saliva is thought to numb the skin as well.
In a single meal, an adult medicinal leech can drink up to ten times its own weight.
The Hebrew word in Proverbs 30:15 appears only once in the Bible and comes from a root linked to “sucking.”
:::

### The target is blood

The leech’s target is blood, for a few reasons:

1. **Blood stands for vitality.** A body without blood is pale and weak. “The life of the flesh is in the blood” ([[Lv 17.11|Leviticus 17:11]]).
2. **In hospitals**, blood is needed for transfusions and to keep the patient alive.
3. **The woman with the flow of blood** lost blood for twelve years: her life was slipping away little by little ([[Mc 5.25-29|Mark 5:25-29]]).
4. **Blood stands for protection.** In Egypt, the houses that had the blood were spared ([[Êx 12.13|Exodus 12:13]]).

## The leech is like sin: it never says enough

### Sin sucks away spiritual sensitivity

Paul describes those who have reached that point: their understanding is darkened, they are alienated from the life of God, and they have “become callous” ([[Ef 4.18-19|Ephesians 4:18-19]]). This is how sin works:

1. **Sin sucks away your freedom**, and you become a slave ([[Jo 8.34|John 8:34]]).
2. **Sin takes away your communion with God** ([[Is 59.2|Isaiah 59:2]]).
3. **Sin sucks away your hope** and leads to death ([[Rm 6.23|Romans 6:23]]).
4. **Sin drags you to disaster.** “Deep calls to deep” ([[Sl 42.7|Psalm 42:7]]).

### Learning to say no

We need to learn to say no to the things that rob us of our vitality. Lemuel’s mother advises her son, who was a king: “Don’t give your strength to women, nor your ways to that which destroys kings” ([[Pv 31.3|Proverbs 31:3]]).

### Desires never say “enough”

The leech stands for human desires, which never say “enough”: it always wants more. Destruction comes in increasing order, not decreasing.

The flesh asks and will never stop asking: drinks, sex, drugs, pleasures, entertainment, and whatever comes next. There are ways that seem right, but their end is death ([[Pv 14.12|Proverbs 14:12]]).

These desires do not bring contentment to the soul. The psalmist said: “My soul thirsts for God” ([[Sl 42.2|Psalm 42:2]]).

### An insatiable figure

The leech wants, and will keep wanting, someone to suck.

- It will never say “that’s enough, I have to change.” It does not know what it means to stop.
- It is greedy and unsatisfied, and it has a desire that is never satisfied and never full. The chapter itself lists other things that are never full: Sheol, the barren womb, the land that never has enough water, and fire ([[Pv 30.15-16|Proverbs 30:15-16]]).

James shows how desire works:

Man is drawn away. Lot was drawn by the well-watered plain and ended up pitching his tents as far as Sodom ([[Gn 13.10-12|Genesis 13:10-12]]). Samson was drawn by a Philistine woman ([[Jz 14.1-3|Judges 14:1-3]]). And you?

But on the last day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried out: “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!” ([[Jo 7.37|John 7:37]]).

Whoever thirsts to overcome, let them come. Whoever wants to be different, let them come. Whoever longs for God, let them come.
